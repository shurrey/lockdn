# AI Tutor

Lockdn's AI tutor acts like a wise mentor — helping you understand concepts without just giving you answers. This guide explains how to get the most from your AI tutor.

---

## How the Tutor Works

The AI tutor is designed to help you learn, not to do your homework for you. When you ask questions:

- It guides you toward understanding
- It asks clarifying questions
- It breaks down complex concepts
- It connects new ideas to what you already know

The tutor knows about your courses, assignments, and notes, so it can provide context-aware help.

---

## Starting a Conversation

### Opening the Tutor

1. Click **Tutor** in the main menu
2. Select a course to focus on (optional)
3. Start typing your question

### Focusing on a Course

When you select a course:

- The tutor has context about that subject
- It can reference your notes for that course
- It knows your upcoming deadlines
- Explanations are tailored to your course level

Leave the course unselected for general questions.

---

## Asking Good Questions

### Be Specific

Instead of "I don't understand Chapter 5," try:

> "I'm confused about how photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy. Can you explain the light-dependent reactions?"

### Share Context

The more context you provide, the better help you'll get:

- What you're working on
- What you've already tried
- Where specifically you're stuck

### Use Images

You can share images with the tutor:

1. Click the image icon 📷 in the chat
2. Select an image (homework problem, diagram, etc.)
3. Ask your question about the image

> **Tip:** This is great for asking about specific homework problems or diagrams from your textbook.

---

## Understanding Tutor Modes

The tutor adapts its behavior based on what you need.

### Learning Mode

When you're trying to understand a concept:

- Detailed explanations
- Multiple examples
- Follow-up questions to check understanding
- Connections to related topics

### Homework Help Mode

When you're working on an assignment:

- Socratic questioning (guides you to the answer)
- Hints rather than direct answers
- Explanation of methods and approaches
- Encouragement to try things yourself

> **Note:** The tutor intentionally doesn't give direct answers to homework. This helps you actually learn the material and maintains academic integrity.

## Getting Better Results

### Iterating on Answers

If an explanation doesn't click:

- Ask for a different example
- Request an analogy
- Ask to break it down further
- Say what specifically is confusing

Example:
> "That makes sense, but I'm still confused about how the electrons move. Can you explain that part with a different analogy?"

### Asking for Specific Formats

Request the format that works best for you:

- "Can you explain this as a step-by-step process?"
- "Can you give me a visual way to think about this?"
- "Can you show me how this applies to a real example?"

### Testing Your Understanding

After learning something:

> "Can you ask me a question to check if I understand this correctly?"

The tutor will quiz you and provide feedback.

## Academic Integrity

The tutor is designed to maintain academic integrity:

### What the Tutor Will Do

- Explain concepts and methods
- Work through example problems (not your homework)
- Guide you toward answers with questions
- Check your understanding
- Provide practice problems

### What the Tutor Won't Do

- Give direct answers to homework problems
- Write essays or papers for you
- Solve specific assignment problems
- Provide answers you can copy

### Why This Matters

- You actually learn the material
- You're prepared for exams
- You develop problem-solving skills
- Your grades reflect real understanding

## Tips for Success

### Before Talking to the Tutor

1. Try the problem yourself first
2. Identify exactly where you're stuck
3. Review your notes on the topic
4. Formulate a specific question

### During the Conversation

1. Engage with follow-up questions
2. Try examples the tutor suggests
3. Say when you understand (or don't)
4. Take notes on key insights

### After the Conversation

1. Review what you learned
2. Try similar problems on your own
3. Generate practice questions on the topic
4. Return if you get stuck again

## Common Scenarios

### "I need to understand a concept for an exam"

> "I have an exam on thermodynamics next week. Can you help me understand entropy? I get that it's about disorder, but I don't understand how to apply it to problems."

### "I'm stuck on how to approach a problem"

> "I need to solve an optimization problem for calculus, but I don't know where to start. Can you guide me through the general approach without solving my specific problem?"

### "I want to review before a test"

> "Can you quiz me on the key concepts from Chapter 3 of my Biology course? Focus on cell division and mitosis."

### "I don't understand my professor's explanation"

> "My professor explained recursion in programming, but I didn't follow. Can you explain it differently, maybe with a simple example?"

## Troubleshooting

### "The tutor isn't answering my question"

- Rephrase your question more specifically
- Provide more context
- Check that you have an AI provider configured in Settings

### "The answers seem generic"

- Select your course for course-specific context
- Reference your notes in the question
- Be more specific about what you're studying

### "I need a direct answer for time-sensitive work"

The tutor is designed for learning, not quick answers. For urgent situations, it can explain methods you can apply yourself.
